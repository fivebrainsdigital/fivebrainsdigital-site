The Gray Age

The Gray Age

The decisions and paperwork of later life, in plain English — whether it is yours or your parents’.

Nobody trains for this. It usually starts with a fall, a phone call, a form or a diagnosis, and within a week there are deadlines and decisions nobody ever explained.

These guides cover the parts families actually get stuck on. Each one is set in 16-point type — larger than an ordinary paperback, deliberately, because you may be reading it tired, in bad light, without your glasses. Most work two ways: print it and write on the lines with a pen, or type onto the same lines on a phone or laptop. Two are print only. Every figure carries the year it applies to, and every claim names the body that published it.

They are general information, not advice about your own situation, and they say so.

Questions people ask

Where do I start when a parent suddenly needs care?

The First 90 Days is a starter binder for new caregivers: what to gather, who to call, what to write down, and what can wait. If it started with a hospital stay, begin with our free guide, "Admitted, or Observation?" If someone is in danger or needs urgent medical care, call 911 first.

Is there a checklist for caring for aging parents?

The Decades Ahead is a decade-by-decade checklist of what comes due at 55, 65, 75 and 80. The First 90 Days is a starter binder for your first three months as a caregiver.

Why are these guides priced so low?

On purpose. Each guide is five to ten dollars. We keep them low because many families reading them are already paying for care.

Are the guides easy to read?

The main text is set in 16-point type and written in plain English. Print one and write on the lines with a pen.

Is this legal, medical or financial advice?

No. This is general information about published rules and procedures. It is not medical, legal, financial or tax advice. Only a clinician can diagnose a condition. For your own situation, talk to a licensed professional in your state.
