The Scam Sheet

What is going around right now

A free newsletter about the scams actually in circulation — how each one works, the tells that give it away, and what to do if it has already happened.

It is written for the people being targeted and for the families who worry about them, not for security professionals. Each issue takes one scam, or two related ones, and covers it properly: the mechanics, the red flags, and a recovery walkthrough by payment method — wire, card, gift card, crypto, payment app — plus where to report it.

Its numbers come from public agencies such as the FTC, the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, Medicare and state attorneys general, and each one is credited in the text. There are no affiliate links and no sponsored content.

Questions people ask

Does Medicare call people and ask for their Medicare number?

Medicare says it will never call you uninvited and ask for your personal information. It only calls and asks for that information in a few cases, like calling you back after you left a message or reported fraud. If a caller says they are Medicare and asks for your number, hang up and call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) yourself. If you are in a Medicare Advantage or drug plan, your own plan can call you, and so can the agent who helped you join. If you are not sure who is calling, hang up and call your plan yourself.

Why do Medicare scams show up in the fall?

Medicare Open Enrollment runs October 15 to December 7 each year. The FTC says scammers get more active every year around Open Enrollment, trying to get your money, your information, or both. There are other enrollment periods too, like January 1 to March 31 for people already in a Medicare Advantage plan, so stay careful all year.

What should I do if I already gave my Medicare number to a caller?

Call 1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227) and tell them someone may be using your Medicare number. You can ask for a new number. If you have Original Medicare, check your Medicare Summary Notices for anything you did not get. If you are in a plan, check what your plan sends you the same way. Your Senior Medicare Patrol can help: 1-877-808-2468 or smpresource.org. You can also report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and for identity theft, go to IdentityTheft.gov.
