The Scam Sheet

What is going around right now

A free newsletter about the scams actually in circulation — how each one works, the tells that give it away, and what to do if it has already happened.

It is written for the people being targeted and for the families who worry about them, not for security professionals. Each issue takes one scam, or two related ones, and covers it properly: the mechanics, the red flags, and a recovery walkthrough by payment method — wire, card, gift card, crypto, payment app — plus where to report it.

Every number in it comes from the FTC, the FBI's IC3, AARP, the Better Business Bureau or a state attorney general, and it is named. Nothing is estimated. There are no affiliate links and no sponsored content.

Read it and subscribe — free

What has been covered

  • Imposter scams — the caller who says they are your bank, the government, or a company you use.
  • Romance and friendship scams — the slow ones, where money is never mentioned early.
  • Fake social-media shopping ads and recovery scams — a connected pair, because people caught by the first often get targeted by the second.
The Scam Sheet reports publicly documented fraud patterns and official guidance. It is general information, not legal or financial advice, and it cannot tell you whether a particular message you received is fraudulent. If you believe you have been targeted, report it at reportfraud.ftc.gov or ic3.gov.
